Fellowships

Understand research fellowships, international fellowships and exchange programs, including fit, funding, motivation and application documents.

Fellowships vary widely. Some are short research placements, some support graduate study, some fund professional development and others are structured exchange programs. The label alone does not tell you the level, duration or funding. Start by reading the project description and the profile of the person the program is trying to recruit.

Research fellowships

Research fellowships are often built around a defined scientific question, research group or training theme. Fit matters because selection committees want to see that the applicant understands the project and has a realistic reason for joining it.

For interdisciplinary projects, transferable skills can be important. A computer science student may fit a computational biology fellowship if the project welcomes programming backgrounds and the applicant can show genuine motivation to learn the biological context.

International fellowships

International programs may combine research, leadership, study, networking or professional development. Read the goals of the fellowship before writing your motivation letter. A statement focused only on personal benefit can miss the program's broader purpose.

Check travel, insurance, visa and accommodation arrangements. International opportunities sometimes fund these costs separately or reimburse them after arrival.

Exchange programs

Exchange programs are usually connected to a home institution, partner network or bilateral agreement. Credits, tuition responsibilities and nomination processes can be as important as academic eligibility.

Before applying, confirm whether your home university must nominate you and whether the courses or research period can be recognized after you return.

How to read a fellowship project description

Project descriptions often reveal the selection criteria indirectly. Notice the methods, research question, expected background and phrases such as “preferred,” “required” or “no prior experience necessary.” Build your application around what is explicitly requested rather than guessing what the committee wants.

If a project says a strong motivation to learn is essential, demonstrate that motivation through concrete examples of learning new tools or moving across disciplines, not by repeating the word “passionate.”

Motivation letter strategy

A strong fellowship motivation letter usually has a clear narrative: your background, the experience that prepared you, the reason this specific project matters to you, what you can contribute and what you need to learn. It should read like a human explanation of fit, not a list of adjectives.

Do not copy the project description into your letter. Use its terminology only where it helps explain the connection between the project and your own experience.

Final fellowship checklist

Before emailing or submitting, confirm the exact addressee, required documents, file format and deadline. Research fellowships may ask for a CV, motivation letter, transcript and reference letter, while others use an online form.

  • Correct project title and lab or program name
  • Tailored motivation letter
  • Relevant CV
  • Transcript if requested
  • Reference letter or referee details if requested
  • Clear file names
  • Correct recipient and subject line
  • Official deadline checked on the provider website

How interdisciplinary applicants can present themselves

Many modern fellowship projects sit between disciplines. A project in computational biology may invite computer science, physics, mathematics or engineering students because the research requires programming and modelling alongside biological knowledge. In such cases, the strongest application does not pretend that the applicant already knows everything. It explains which skills transfer directly and which new concepts the applicant is motivated to learn.

For example, experience in Python, machine learning, simulation or data analysis can be relevant to a project that studies biological systems computationally. The motivation letter should then explain why the biological question is interesting, rather than simply saying that the applicant wants to use machine learning somewhere new.

Reading the people section of a fellowship call

If a project is hosted in a named laboratory, research group or department, read the official lab page before applying. Understand the broad research direction and the role of the people named in the call. This helps you address the correct person and write a more informed explanation of fit.

Do not over-personalize the letter with claims about a researcher's work that you have not actually read. A simple and accurate connection between your interests and the project description is better than exaggerated praise.

Email applications

When a fellowship asks applicants to email documents, use a clear subject line and a short professional message. Attach only the requested files, use readable file names and check that the files open before sending. The email itself does not need to repeat the entire motivation letter. Its purpose is to identify the application, summarize your background in a few lines and list the attached documents.

Preparing a research-focused CV

For a research fellowship, your CV should make technical and academic evidence easy to see. Education, relevant programming or analytical skills, final-year projects, research interests and any genuine research experience can be more useful than a generic professional summary. If you include a portfolio or GitHub link, make sure the linked work is public and understandable.

When the fellowship project names methods you have not used, do not list them as skills. Instead, use the motivation letter to explain related experience and your willingness to learn the new method. Accuracy protects your credibility during an interview.

Preparing for a fellowship interview

Be ready to explain why the project interests you in your own words. Review the project description, the host lab's broad research area and every technical claim in your CV. Interviewers may ask how your previous project relates to the fellowship or how you would approach a topic you have not studied before.

A thoughtful answer can acknowledge uncertainty and describe how you would learn. Research selection does not require pretending to know everything; it often values the ability to reason, ask useful questions and learn unfamiliar material.
